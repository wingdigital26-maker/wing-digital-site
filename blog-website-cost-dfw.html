How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business in DFW?

What agencies charge, what a DIY builder really costs you in hours, and the question worth asking any quote.

By Jack Wing, Wing Digital Published July 28, 2026 Updated September 7, 2026 All posts →
Asking what a website costs gets you more different answers than asking five contractors to look at the same job.

There is no single number, and anyone who gives you one before asking a question is reading off a template. The same site can cost you almost nothing upfront and a year of your evenings, or a large project fee and a two month wait, or a flat monthly rate. What separates those three is not the quality of the design. It is who maintains the thing after launch, and what happens the first time you need to change a price on it. Here is the honest version of each, no sales math.

How much does a DIY website builder really cost?

On paper, a builder like Wix, Squarespace, or GoDaddy runs from free to about $50 a month. That is the number everyone quotes. The number nobody quotes is your time. The owners we talk to who went this route tell us the same story almost word for word: weeks of evenings and weekends, and a site they still quietly apologize for when they hand out the address.

A second cost shows up months later. Google reads structure, page speed, and markup before it ever reads your paragraphs, and Google's own SEO starter guide warns that even after you do the work, "some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months." A drag-and-drop template does not set that foundation up for you, so the DIY site launches, looks fine on your laptop, and then never appears when a customer actually searches. A website nobody can find is not a cheap website. It is a $50 a month business card that happens to live on the internet.

DIY genuinely makes sense for a brand-new side business testing an idea, and we will tell you so on a call. For a company that needs the phone to ring, the hours you pour in are usually worth more than the money you save.

What do traditional agencies charge in DFW?

More than most owners expect, and the quote is usually a project fee with a number of hours behind it rather than a price off a menu. For that money you often do get a genuinely professional result, and if you have the budget and the patience, an agency can be exactly the right call.

The catch is the model, not the quality. The fee is due mostly upfront, the timeline commonly runs six to twelve weeks, and after launch almost every change comes with an hourly invoice. Swap a photo or update a price in March and that is a billable request, which is how a site quietly stops getting updated by June. We have nothing against agencies, we are one, but upfront-and-hourly was built for a company with a marketing manager on staff, not for an owner who is on a roof or under a sink all day and needs the website to keep up with the business.

How does the monthly model work, and why is it cheaper over time?

Our version is one flat monthly rate once the site is live. It covers the design, the copywriting, your domain and hosting, and every small update after launch. The reason it usually wins on total cost has nothing to do with the sticker price and everything to do with month six.

An upfront site is finished the day it launches, and it starts aging that same day. Prices drift out of date, photos go stale, a service you dropped two winters ago is still listed, and Google slowly works out that nobody is home. A monthly site gets those things fixed as they happen, plus ongoing on-page SEO, because keeping it current and ranking is what the monthly rate pays for. So the question worth asking a quote is not "what is the upfront price." It is "what does this cost me over two years, and who is touching it in month fourteen." That is the whole idea behind our web development service.

What actually determines the price of a website?

Four things move the number, and everything else is small next to them:

  • Page count. A tidy three-page site costs far less than a fifteen-page build with a dedicated page for every suburb you serve. More pages also means more searches you can win, which is why we usually grow a site over time instead of launching everything at once.
  • Copywriting. The single most skipped line item, and the one that decides whether a visitor calls. We write every word ourselves, because "insert text here" has never made a phone ring, and generic filler quietly tells a customer you did not care enough to explain what you do.
  • SEO foundation. Schema, page speed, and clean structure decide whether Google ever sends you a single visitor. We build it in from day one, since bolting it on after launch always costs more than doing it right the first time.
  • Maintenance. A site nobody updates stops winning work inside a year. We have quietly inherited enough abandoned websites to say that with a straight face.
