Why Your Business Isn't Showing Up on Google Maps

By Jack Wing, Wing Digital · Published July 28, 2026

Being invisible on Google Maps is like owning a work truck with no lettering. You technically exist, but nobody driving past will ever know it.

If your business is not showing up on Google Maps, it is almost always one of five problems: an unclaimed or incomplete profile, the wrong categories, too few recent reviews, a weak website behind the profile, or business information that does not match across the web. The good news is that every one of them is fixable, and most within a month. We audit DFW profiles on our free consultations, and these five cover very nearly everything we find. Here is how to spot and fix each one.

Is your profile unclaimed or half-empty?

Start here, because it is the most common cause by a wide margin. Google will build a bare profile for a business from scraps of public data, and it ranks those unclaimed or half-finished profiles well behind complete ones. If you have never formally claimed yours, or you claimed it and stopped halfway, that alone can keep you off the map.

The fix is straightforward: claim your profile, then fill in every single field. Services, hours, service areas, a real description, and at least ten photos. The profiles we audit almost always stalled at the same point, name and phone entered and then abandoned, and to Google that reads as a business nobody is minding. Twenty focused minutes of filling in fields is the cheapest ranking gain available in local search.

Are your categories wrong?

Your primary category is the single strongest ranking signal you directly control, and it is quietly wrong on a huge number of trade businesses. "Roofing contractor" and "General contractor" rank for completely different searches, and if you picked the broad one years ago because it felt safer, you have been invisible for your actual money search ever since.

Google's own category guidelines are clear: choose the most specific primary category that describes your core business, then add every secondary category that honestly applies. We see category fixes move a profile more than a full month of any other work, because you are finally telling Google the one thing it cares about most, which is what you actually do.

Do you have enough recent reviews?

Google weighs review count, average rating, and recency together, and recency is the piece owners forget. Twelve reviews from two years ago lose to a competitor collecting three a week right now, because a stale review page signals a business that has slowed down even if you are busier than ever.

The fix is a system, not a sticky note: an automatic review request sent the moment a job is complete, ideally straight from your CRM so it never depends on anyone remembering at six on a Friday. This is step four of our local SEO playbook, and it is the change clients tend to feel the fastest, because a steady drip of fresh five-star reviews lifts both your ranking and the trust of the person reading them.

Is the website behind your profile weak?

People forget the map profile and the website are graded together. Google checks the site behind your profile to verify what you do and where you do it, and a one-page site that just says "quality service since 2015" gives it nothing to confirm. A proper website with real service pages and city pages lifts the map ranking right along with it.

Think of the profile and the website as one application with two halves. When the profile says "roof repair in Plano" and the website has an actual roof repair page and an actual Plano page backing it up, Google can trust both and rank both. When the site is a single thin page, it can confidently trust neither, and your map ranking pays for it.

Does your business information match across the web?

Your name, address, and phone number need to be identical everywhere they appear, on Google, Yelp, Facebook, and your own website, right down to the suite number and whether you wrote "Street" or "St." When those details disagree across the web, Google loses confidence that any single version is correct, and it hedges by ranking you lower everywhere.

This one is boring and it quietly works. Pick one exact format for your name, address, and phone, write it down as the master version, and then fix every listing to match it precisely. It is an afternoon of tedious cleanup, and it firms up the foundation under everything else on this list.

Frequently asked questions

How long until my Maps ranking improves?
Profile fixes can show within 2 to 4 weeks. Reviews and website improvements compound over 2 to 3 months.

Should I pay for Google Ads on Maps?
Ads buy instant placement but stop when the budget does. Fix the organic ranking first, then decide whether ads still make sense.

Do I need a physical address to rank on Maps?
No. Service-area businesses can hide their address and list the cities they serve instead, which is the right setup for most home service companies.

Why did my ranking drop suddenly?
Usually a profile suspension, a competitor surge in reviews, or a Google update. Check the profile status first, then review velocity.
