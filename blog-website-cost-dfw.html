How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business in DFW?

By Jack Wing, Wing Digital · Published July 28, 2026

What a Website Really Costs

Asking what a website costs gets you more different answers than asking five contractors to look at the same job.

In 2026, a professional small business website in Dallas-Fort Worth costs anywhere from nothing upfront with a DIY builder to more than $10,000 from a traditional agency. We run it on one flat monthly rate with hosting, updates, and support included, quoted on a free 15-minute call. That spread is huge, so the real work is understanding what you actually get at each price, and what it costs you later. Below is the honest version, no sales math.

How much does a DIY website builder really cost?

On paper, a builder like Wix, Squarespace, or GoDaddy runs from free to about $50 a month. That is the number everyone quotes. The number nobody quotes is your time. The owners we talk to who went this route tell us the same story almost word for word: thirty, forty, sometimes sixty hours of evenings and weekends, and a site they still quietly apologize for when they hand out the address.

There is a second cost that shows up months later. Google reads structure, page speed, and schema markup before it ever reads your paragraphs, and Google's own SEO starter guide lists dozens of technical items a drag-and-drop template simply never sets up. So the DIY site launches, looks fine on your laptop, and then never appears when a customer actually searches. A website nobody can find is not a cheap website. It is a $50 a month business card that happens to live on the internet.

DIY can genuinely make sense for a brand-new side business testing an idea. For a real company that needs the phone to ring, the hours you pour in are usually worth more than the money you save.

What do traditional agencies charge in DFW?

A lot more than most owners expect. Forbes Advisor puts a typical agency build between $10,000 and $35,000, with freelancers commonly landing anywhere from $1,500 to $5,000. For that money you usually do get a genuinely professional result, and if you have the budget and the patience, an agency can be the right call.

The catch is the model, not the quality. The big number is due upfront, the timeline usually runs six to twelve weeks, and after launch almost every change comes with an hourly invoice. Want to swap a photo or update a price in March? That is a billable request. We have nothing against agencies, we are one, but the upfront-and-hourly model was built for companies with a marketing manager on staff, not for an owner who is on a roof or under a sink all day and just needs the website to work.

How does the monthly model work, and why is it cheaper over time?

Our version is one flat monthly rate once the site is live. It covers the design, the copywriting, your domain and hosting, and every small update after launch. The reason it usually wins on total cost has nothing to do with the sticker price and everything to do with month six.

An upfront site is finished the day it launches, and it starts aging that same day. Prices drift out of date, photos get stale, a service you dropped is still listed, and Google slowly notices nobody is home. A monthly site gets those things fixed as they happen, plus ongoing on-page SEO, because keeping it current and ranking is literally what the monthly rate pays for. Most small business sites end up costing somewhere between $2,000 and $15,000 once you add real features and a year of upkeep, so the question that actually matters is not "what is the upfront price," it is "what does this cost me over two years, and is anyone maintaining it." That thinking is the whole idea behind our website design service.

What actually determines the price of a website?

Four things move the number, and everything else is small next to them:

  • Page count. A tidy three-page site costs far less than a fifteen-page build with a dedicated page for every suburb you serve. More pages also means more searches you can win, which is why we usually grow a site over time instead of launching everything at once.
  • Copywriting. The single most skipped line item, and the one that decides whether a visitor actually calls. We write every word ourselves, because "insert text here" has never made a phone ring, and generic filler quietly tells a customer you did not care enough to explain what you do.
  • SEO foundation. Schema, page speed, and clean structure decide whether Google ever sends you a single visitor. We build it in from day one, since bolting it on after launch always costs more than doing it right the first time.
  • Maintenance. A site nobody updates stops winning work inside a year. We have quietly inherited enough abandoned websites to say that with a straight face.
