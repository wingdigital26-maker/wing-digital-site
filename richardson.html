The Richardson problem01 / 04

A city with a corporate corridor down the middle of it.

The stretch of Highway 75 through Richardson, from around Arapaho up to Renner, has been called the telecom corridor for decades, and the office density along it, plus the campuses at CityLine by the Bush Turnpike and the university on the north edge, changes who your customer is. A great many Richardson businesses sell to other businesses: facilities managers, office managers, purchasing people, operations leads. Those buyers behave nothing like a homeowner with a leak.

They shortlist quietly. They read three websites, work out whether you are the size of company they can risk, look for evidence you have done this before, and send one email. There is no phone call at the top of that process, and quite often no phone call at all until the decision is nearly made. A site that is really a brochure with a contact form loses that comparison without ever knowing it happened.

The rest of Richardson is a different job again. South and west of Belt Line the housing is decades older than anything in Frisco or Prosper, which produces steady demand for the trades that repair rather than install, and the strip of independent restaurants and shops around Greenville and Arapaho competes on reputation and photographs more than on ranking. One city, two entirely separate briefs, and the pages have to know which one you are.

Proof, not promises02 / 04

The part of this we can already show you.

Jackson Roofing, a family roofing company in Plano, is the build closest to this argument, because most of what we do for them is aimed at other businesses.

Alongside the search work, the site and the content, we research and maintain a list of North Texas businesses that hire roofers. Richardson holds 43 of them. That figure is the number of prospects on one client's list in this city. It is not a market size, and it is not a count of Wing clients here, because there are none here yet.

Everything on that list is contacted by drafted, queued messages that a person approves. Nothing sends by itself, which matters more when the recipient is a company than when it is a homeowner.
