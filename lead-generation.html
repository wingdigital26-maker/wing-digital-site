Lead Generation

How we fill the pipeline and keep every lead warm

Two things kill a local business pipeline. Referrals go quiet for six weeks with no warning, and the leads you do get slip away because somebody else answered first. This service handles both halves: we go find the work, and we make sure not one lead that comes in ever sits there cooling off.

What does this service include?

On the outreach side, we build a targeted list of the businesses or homeowners who actually fit what you do in the cities you serve, research them so the message can reference something real, and send it in your voice with follow-up that keeps going without being obnoxious.

On the automation side, every lead who contacts you gets an instant reply, then a follow-up sequence spaced over days and weeks, because most people asking for a quote are not ready to book that same afternoon. After a finished job, a review request goes out automatically. Old leads who went cold months ago get a quiet nudge. All of it runs from your CRM without you touching it.

Why does response speed matter this much?

Because a lead is at their hottest in the first few minutes and cools fast. Somebody researching a roofer at 9pm is comparing three companies in one sitting, and the one that answers while they are still looking usually wins. It often has nothing to do with price.

You cannot beat that clock by hand while you are on a ladder or under a sink, which is exactly why it should be automated. The system never gets busy or forgets. It is the same reason we build the website to capture the lead cleanly in the first place, since none of this works if the form does not feed it.

How is this different from buying leads?

Bought leads are sold to four of your competitors at the same time, which is why they turn into a race to the bottom on price. The person on the other end is fielding four calls and picking the cheapest. You are not selling anymore, you are bidding.

What we build is your own pipeline, going to people nobody else in your trade is talking to that week, with your name on it. It takes longer to warm up and it is worth more, because you are the only one in the conversation. It also compounds: the list, the messaging, and the reply data are yours and get sharper every month.

Does automated mean impersonal?

Only if it is written badly. The sequences we write sound like a person because we write them from how you actually talk, and they reference the specific thing the customer asked about. Most people never think twice about it, they just feel like they got a fast, straight answer.

The moment someone replies, the automation stops and it is a real conversation with you. The system exists to start conversations, not to fake them. You approve every sequence before it goes live, and if anything starts reading like spam, we rewrite it.

What about the customers you already have?

This is the part almost every local business is sitting on and not using. You have served hundreds of people over the years, and most of them are in a text thread, a paper invoice, a QuickBooks export, or your head. They already know you, already paid you, and already trust you. They are the cheapest work you will ever win, and right now they are invisible.

So the first thing we do is build the catalog. We pull every past customer out of wherever they are scattered and put them in one place, with the job we did, when we did it, the address, and how to reach them. That list becomes an asset you own, not a pile of records nobody can search.

Then it goes to work on a schedule. Here is what that looks like in practice. A customer finishes a job and is tagged with what they had done. A few days later an automatic review request goes out while they are still happy. At six months, a maintenance or check-in message. At the one year mark, a reminder tied to the season, because a roof inspection matters before spring storms and an AC tune-up matters before July. Anyone who has gone quiet for two years gets a simple we are still here message with an offer worth replying to.

None of that requires you to remember anybody. The catalog remembers, the schedule fires, and you get a call from someone who already likes you. It also feeds everything else: repeat customers leave more reviews, and reviews are one of the strongest signals in local search, so keeping in touch with old customers quietly helps you get found by new ones.

How does this fit with the rest of the system?

Local SEO brings in the people already searching for you. This service reaches the ones who have not started looking yet, and then catches everybody either way. Reviews are the overlap: the automatic review request after each job is also one of the strongest signals in local search, so the follow-up quietly lifts your ranking too.

Run the outreach, the follow-up, and the past-customer catalog together and the pipeline stops depending on luck. You can see the engines behind all of it on our work page.
